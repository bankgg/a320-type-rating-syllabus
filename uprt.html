A320 notes

  • Normal law: the roll rate is proportional to lateral sidestick, up to 15°/s at the stop. Up to 33° the bank holds with the stick neutral; above 33° it returns to 33° when the stick is released, and with full stick held it goes to 67°. A320 notes

  • "15%", "15*" and "150" are read as 15° of bank. Your decision
  • The rudder is not normally used to roll a swept-wing transport. The FCTM says it should not be used to induce roll or to counter turbulence, and that aggressive, full or nearly full, opposite rudder pedal inputs must never be applied: against a sideslip they can exceed the limit loads, even below VA, and the rudder travel limiter does not prevent this. The reversal shown is a moderate input. Session guidance p. 257 (photo) · FCTM AS-RUD P 2/2, PDF 143 · FCTM PR-AEP-MISC P 11/32, PDF 456
  • Alternate law: roll direct, about 30°/s at the stop when clean, and yaw damping only, limited to ±5° of rudder, with no turn coordination. In pitch, alternate law handles as normal law inside the normal envelope, so the sidestick stays neutral. FCOM DSC-27-20-20 P 12/20, PDF 1623 · FCOM DSC-27-20-20 P 17/20, PDF 1628 · FCTM AOP-10-30-20 P 1/2, PDF 62
  • STOP RUDDER INPUT (red PFD message, aural and MASTER WARN) triggers on inappropriate rudder pedal inputs in cruise at high speed. The books give no thresholds, and it is not shown here. FCOM DSC-31-40 P 53/54, PDF 1944 · FCOM DSC-31-10 P 8/16, PDF 1829 · FCTM AS-RUD P 2/2, PDF 143

A320 notes

  • Direct law: elevator deflection is proportional to sidestick deflection and there is no automatic trim, so USE MAN PITCH TRIM shows on the PFD. The pitch trim wheels drive the stabilizer mechanically, with priority over electrical trim.   • The sidesticks are spring-loaded to neutral and get no feedback from the control surfaces. On the A320 the "pitch forces" of the guidance show as how far the sidestick must be held to keep the attitude. FCOM DSC-27-20-30 P 4/22, PDF 1635 · Session guidance p. 257 (photo)

A320 notes

  • The guidance mentions PRIM 1+2+3, but the A320 has no PRIMs: SEC 1, 2 and 3 control its spoilers. On HS-TXA the speedbrakes are spoilers 2, 3 and 4. Extension is inhibited when SEC 1 and SEC 3 are both faulty, an elevator is faulty, the angle-of-attack protection is active, the flaps are at FULL, a thrust lever is above MCT, or alpha floor is active. None of these applies here. Session guidance p. 258 (photo) · FCOM DSC-27-10-10 P 3/6, PDF 1562 · FCOM DSC-27-10-20 P 7/16, PDF 1572
  • Speedbrakes pitch the nose up; the AP and normal law compensate, but in direct law the pilot must. Avoid sudden speedbrake movements in direct law: with the speedbrakes out and the aircraft re-trimmed, retract them gently to give time to re-trim and avoid a large nose-down trim change. FCTM PR-AEP-MISC P 14/32, PDF 459 · FCTM AOP-10-30-20 P 1/2, PDF 62
  • F/CTL DIRECT LAW procedure: at high Mach numbers, use the speedbrakes with care to avoid too strong nose-up changes. FCOM PRO-ABN-F_CTL P 19/132, PDF 3492

A320 notes

  • Flown with AP2 in HDG and ALT with the flight director bars shown. Your decision
  • With the AP engaged the FMGS sends the rudder trim orders, which compensate an engine failure over the longer term in every guidance mode. The rudder trim moves the neutral point of the pedal feel, so the pedals move with it; yaw damping and turn coordination do not move the pedals.
